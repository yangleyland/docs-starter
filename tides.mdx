## Overview

Ocean tides are the regular rise and fall of sea level. They are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the Moon and, to a lesser extent, the Sun.

## What causes tides

The Moon's gravity pulls on Earth's water. This creates a bulge of water on the side of Earth facing the Moon.

A second bulge forms on the opposite side of Earth. There, the Moon's pull on the water is weaker than its pull on the rest of the planet, so the water is left behind. Earth's rotation carries each location through both bulges every day.

## High and low tide

- **High tide:** A location is inside a bulge, and sea level is at its highest.
- **Low tide:** A location is between bulges, and sea level is at its lowest.

Most coastlines experience two high tides and two low tides each lunar day, which lasts about 24 hours and 50 minutes.

## Spring and neap tides

The Sun's gravity also affects tides. Its effect changes with the Moon's position relative to the Sun and Earth.

- **Spring tides:** At new moon and full moon, the Sun, Moon, and Earth line up. Their pulls combine, and the tidal range is largest.
- **Neap tides:** At the first and last quarter moons, the Sun and Moon pull at right angles. Their pulls partly cancel, and the tidal range is smallest.

## Local factors

Coastline shape, water depth, and ocean basin size change the height and timing of tides at a given location.
