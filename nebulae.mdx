---
title: "Nebulae"
description: "How clouds of gas and dust in space form"
---

A nebula is a cloud of gas and dust in space. Most nebulae are made mostly of hydrogen and helium.

## Nebulae from dying stars

- **Supernova remnants:** A massive star explodes at the end of its life. The blast throws its outer layers into space, where they form an expanding cloud.
- **Planetary nebulae:** A low-mass star sheds its outer layers as it dies. The hot core left behind makes the shed gas glow.

## Nebulae from the interstellar medium

Gravity pulls gas and dust in the space between stars into dense clouds. Nearby young, hot stars can light these clouds up. These clouds are called emission nebulae. If the cloud is dense enough, gravity can collapse parts of it and form new stars.

## Stellar nurseries

Many nebulae are stellar nurseries. As a region collapses, it heats up, and a new star forms at its center. The new star's radiation and winds can then blow away the remaining gas.
