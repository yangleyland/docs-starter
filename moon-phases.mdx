## Why the moon has phases

The moon orbits Earth about every 29.5 days. The sun always lights half of the moon. As the moon orbits, you see different amounts of that lit half from Earth.

## The eight phases

| Phase | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| New moon | The moon is not visible. The lit side faces away from Earth. |
| Waxing crescent | A thin sliver of light grows on the right in the Northern Hemisphere. |
| First quarter | The right half is lit in the Northern Hemisphere. |
| Waxing gibbous | More than half is lit and the lit area keeps growing. |
| Full moon | The entire face is lit. |
| Waning gibbous | More than half is lit and the lit area shrinks. |
| Last quarter | The left half is lit in the Northern Hemisphere. |
| Waning crescent | A thin sliver of light remains on the left in the Northern Hemisphere. |

<Note>
  In the Southern Hemisphere, the lit and dark sides appear reversed.
</Note>

## Waxing and waning

- **Waxing:** The lit portion grows from new moon to full moon.
- **Waning:** The lit portion shrinks from full moon to new moon.
