---
title: "Eclipses"
description: "How solar and lunar eclipses happen"
---

An eclipse happens when the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up so that one body casts a shadow on another.

## Solar eclipses

A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth. The Moon blocks some or all of the Sun's light. Solar eclipses only occur during a new moon.

| Type | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Total | The Moon fully covers the Sun. |
| Partial | The Moon covers only part of the Sun. |
| Annular | The Moon is too far away to cover the Sun fully, so a ring of sunlight remains. |

<Warning>
  Never look directly at the Sun. Use certified eclipse glasses or a solar filter.
</Warning>

## Lunar eclipses

A lunar eclipse happens when Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon. Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. Lunar eclipses only occur during a full moon.

| Type | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Total | The Moon passes fully into Earth's darkest shadow and often looks red. |
| Partial | Only part of the Moon enters Earth's darkest shadow. |
| Penumbral | The Moon passes through Earth's faint outer shadow and dims slightly. |

## Why eclipses are not monthly

The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees relative to Earth's orbit around the Sun. Most months, the Moon passes above or below the line between the Sun and Earth. Eclipses occur only when the alignment happens near the points where the two orbits cross.
