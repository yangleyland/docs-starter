---
title: "Comets"
description: "What comets are made of and how their tails form"
---

Comets are small bodies of ice, dust, and rock that orbit the Sun. Astronomers often describe them as "dirty snowballs" or "icy dirtballs."

## Nucleus

The nucleus is the solid core of a comet. It is typically a few kilometers across and contains:

- **Frozen water**, which makes up most of the ice.
- **Frozen gases**, such as carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, methane, and ammonia.
- **Dust and rock**, including silicate minerals and carbon-rich organic compounds.

## Coma

As a comet nears the Sun, heat turns its ice directly into gas. This process is called sublimation. The gas and freed dust form a glowing cloud around the nucleus called the coma, which can grow much larger than the nucleus itself.

## Tails

Sunlight and the solar wind push material away from the coma and form two tails:

- **Dust tail**: Curved and yellowish-white. It reflects sunlight.
- **Ion tail**: Straight and bluish. It is made of charged gas that glows and points directly away from the Sun.

Both tails point away from the Sun, even when the comet is moving away from it.
